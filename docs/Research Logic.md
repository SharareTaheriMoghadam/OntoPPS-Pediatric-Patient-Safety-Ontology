# Ontology Evaluation Framework

## Overview

The Onto_PPS research adopted a multi-dimensional evaluation framework.

Because ontology quality cannot be adequately assessed through a single metric, complementary evaluation approaches were used to examine content, expert acceptability, comparative coverage, and structural quality.

---

## 1. Expert-Based Evaluation

Domain experts were involved in assessing the ontology according to structured evaluation criteria.

This evaluation examined aspects such as:

- conceptual relevance;
- domain appropriateness;
- clarity;
- usefulness;
- organization;
- applicability to pediatric patient safety.

Expert-based evaluation provided a human and domain-oriented assessment of the ontology.

---

## 2. Literature-Based Content Coverage

The ontology was evaluated against concepts identified from relevant pediatric patient safety literature.

This approach examined how effectively the ontology represented concepts identified in the scientific literature.

The evaluation focused on:

- concept coverage;
- representation of relevant patient safety knowledge;
- identification of potential knowledge gaps.

---

## 3. Comparative Evaluation

The ontology was compared with relevant existing patient safety classifications, taxonomies, and ontologies.

The comparison considered characteristics including:

- conceptual scope;
- number and organization of concepts;
- semantic relationships;
- hierarchical structure;
- domain coverage;
- alignment with existing knowledge systems.

This evaluation supported the assessment of the ontology's contribution in relation to existing patient safety knowledge representation systems.

---

## 4. Computational and Structural Evaluation

Computational methods were used to investigate structural properties of the ontology.

The evaluation examined characteristics including:

- consistency;
- coherence;
- structural organization;
- logical quality.

This component complemented the human and content-based evaluation approaches.

---
