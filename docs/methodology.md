# Research Methodology

## Overview

The Onto_PPS doctoral research followed a structured methodology combining systematic evidence synthesis, patient safety knowledge extraction, ontology engineering, knowledge integration, and multi-dimensional ontology evaluation.

The methodology was designed to address the fragmentation and heterogeneity of patient safety knowledge in pediatric healthcare.

---

## 1. Investigation of Existing Patient Safety Knowledge Systems

The research initially investigated existing patient safety classifications, taxonomies, and ontologies.

The investigation focused on:

- development methodologies;
- evaluation methodologies;
- conceptual scope;
- domain coverage;
- structural characteristics;
- knowledge representation approaches;
- semantic relationships between concepts.

This phase established the methodological and conceptual foundation for the development of a pediatric patient safety ontology.

---

## 2. Systematic Evidence Synthesis

A systematic review approach was used to identify and analyse relevant literature related to patient safety classifications, taxonomies, and ontologies.

The research examined the existing landscape of patient safety knowledge representation systems and identified gaps in:

- methodological standardization;
- conceptual coverage;
- pediatric-specific knowledge representation;
- evaluation approaches.

The findings informed the design requirements and evaluation strategy for the Onto_PPS project.

---

## 3. Pediatric Patient Safety Concept Extraction

Relevant pediatric patient safety literature was systematically investigated to identify concepts associated with patient safety in pediatric healthcare.

The extracted knowledge was analysed and organized to identify:

- patient safety events;
- contributing factors;
- clinical and organizational contexts;
- consequences;
- prevention-related concepts;
- additional dimensions of pediatric patient safety.

The resulting knowledge base supported the conceptualization of the Onto_PPS ontology.

---

## 4. Ontology Engineering

The ontology was developed using established ontology engineering principles.

The development process included:

1. definition of the ontology domain and scope;
2. identification of competency and knowledge requirements;
3. extraction and organization of relevant concepts;
4. development of the conceptual structure;
5. definition of semantic relationships;
6. integration with relevant existing knowledge systems;
7. implementation and formalization;
8. ontology evaluation.

The ontology was developed as a structured representation of knowledge related to pediatric patient safety.

---

## 5. Knowledge Integration and Mapping

The research incorporated knowledge from:

- pediatric patient safety literature;
- existing patient safety classifications;
- taxonomies;
- ontologies;
- healthcare knowledge representation systems.

Mappings and relationships with relevant existing systems were used to support knowledge integration and interoperability.

---

## 6. Multi-Dimensional Evaluation

The ontology was evaluated through complementary approaches, including:

- expert-based assessment;
- literature-based content coverage;
- comparison with existing patient safety knowledge systems;
- computational and structural evaluation.

The combination of these approaches enabled the assessment of both the conceptual content and structural quality of the ontology.

---

## Methodological Contribution

The research demonstrated a structured approach for developing a domain-specific ontology by combining systematic evidence synthesis, knowledge extraction, ontology engineering, knowledge integration, and multi-dimensional evaluation.

This methodological experience provided a foundation for subsequent research in medical ontologies, knowledge graphs, clinical decision support, and healthcare artificial intelligence.
