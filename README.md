Overview


Onto_PPS represents the research portfolio of my doctoral research in Medical Informatics, focused on the development and evaluation of an ontology for pediatric patient safety.


The research addressed the challenge of representing complex, heterogeneous, and fragmented patient safety knowledge in a structured and formally organized manner.


The research programme integrated: systematic evidence synthesis; patient safety knowledge analysis; conceptual knowledge organization; ontology engineering; formal knowledge representation; semantic interoperability; multi-dimensional ontology evaluation.



This repository presents the high-level scientific and methodological foundation of the research.



The Research Problem:
Patient safety knowledge is distributed across diverse classifications, taxonomies, terminologies, conceptual frameworks, and information systems.


This fragmentation creates challenges for: consistent knowledge organization; semantic integration; knowledge reuse; interoperability; computational reasoning; future knowledge-driven healthcare applications.


The Onto_PPS research investigated how ontology engineering and formal knowledge representation could contribute to the structured organization of pediatric patient safety knowledge.

Executive Overview
Onto_PPS represents a doctoral research programme in Medical Informatics focused on the development and multi-dimensional evaluation of a domain-specific ontology for pediatric patient safety.
The research addressed a fundamental challenge in healthcare knowledge representation: patient-safety knowledge is distributed across heterogeneous classifications, taxonomies, ontologies, terminologies, conceptual frameworks, scientific literature, and healthcare information systems.
This fragmentation creates significant challenges for:
consistent knowledge organization;
semantic harmonization;
knowledge integration;
interoperability;
knowledge reuse;
computational reasoning;
and the development of knowledge-driven healthcare applications.
The research therefore investigated how evidence synthesis, systematic knowledge organization, ontology engineering, and formal knowledge representation could be integrated to construct a structured semantic representation of pediatric patient-safety knowledge.

Phase I — Systematic Review of Patient-Safety Classifications and Ontologies
The first phase systematically investigated existing patient-safety classifications and ontological knowledge structures.
Evidence base
88 studies were included.
72 classifications and ontologies were identified and analysed.
The review examined the conceptual landscape of patient-safety knowledge representation and investigated the concepts most frequently represented across existing frameworks.
Among the most frequently represented concepts were:
Incident type
Contributing factors / hazards
This phase established an evidence-based foundation for understanding the structure, scope, overlap, and heterogeneity of existing patient-safety knowledge representations.
Phase II — Pediatric Patient-Safety Knowledge Identification
The second phase focused specifically on pediatric patient safety.
Evidence base
95 pediatric patient-safety articles were included.
2,365 pediatric patient-safety concepts were extracted and analysed.
This phase provided a systematic knowledge base for identifying the breadth and diversity of patient-safety concepts relevant to pediatric healthcare.
The findings demonstrated the complexity of pediatric patient-safety knowledge and provided the empirical foundation for subsequent conceptual organization and ontology development.
Phase III — Onto_PPS Development
The findings from the previous research phases were systematically analysed, compared, integrated, and organized to support the development of Onto_PPS — the Ontology of Pediatric Patient Safety.
The ontology was developed using ontology engineering and formal knowledge-representation principles.

Phase IV — Multi-Dimensional Ontology Evaluation
Onto_PPS was evaluated through multiple complementary approaches designed to examine different dimensions of ontology quality, coverage, validity, and computational characteristics.
The evaluation included:
1. Expert-Oriented Evaluation
The ontology was evaluated through expert assessment, with medical specialists representing multiple domains of pediatric hospital care contributing to the evaluation of its clinical relevance and domain adequacy.
The expert assessment demonstrated generally positive evaluation results, with most evaluation criteria receiving scores above 3.75 out of 5.
2. Literature-Based Coverage Evaluation
A literature-based evaluation examined the extent to which concepts identified in pediatric patient-safety literature were represented within Onto_PPS.
In the evaluation of 26 abstracts, the ontology achieved:
81.2% concept coverage
This provided an empirical assessment of the extent to which Onto_PPS represented concepts identified in relevant pediatric patient-safety literature.
3. Comparative Evaluation
Onto_PPS was compared with existing patient-safety classifications in the pediatric domain.
The comparative assessment examined dimensions including:
conceptual coverage;
number of concepts;
semantic relations;
structural depth;
and mappings with existing knowledge structures.
The comparative findings demonstrated that Onto_PPS provided broader coverage across several structural and knowledge-representation dimensions.
4. Machine-Based Evaluation
A machine-based evaluation approach was also applied to examine computational characteristics of the ontology.
The findings supported the consistency and coherence of Onto_PPS.
Together, these evaluation approaches provided complementary perspectives on:
Clinical Relevance → Content Coverage → Comparative Scope → Structural Organization → Computational Quality
